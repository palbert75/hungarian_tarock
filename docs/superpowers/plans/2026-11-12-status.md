## Bots: what they follow

Your rules, as given:

- Bid only with an honour. XXI always bids, up to "one", never solo. Skíz
  with four or more other tarokks passes; with fewer it bids (up to "two").
  Pagát alone opens only with eight tarokks (up to "two").
- The caller's partner announces four kings with six tarokks and double game
  with five tarokks above the XIII.
- A defender, while no trull is announced and the XXI is not yet played,
  assumes the fellow defender holds the XXI: leads a tarokk if that player
  plays last in the trick, otherwise a suit.
- Tarokks are counted all the time.

Four kings as a signal (your correction): the announcement tells the table
who the partner is, and a declarer with six tarokks and a great honour
answers it with double game.

My simple rules for the rest are in the design, section 5.3. Two changed
during the build because the simulation showed them losing: bots announce
pagát ulti only with nine tarokks (or eight with three at XVII or above),
and trull only with all three honours and seven tarokks.

## What I took from the script and the pagat page

Each idea was a switch, and the same 3,000 deals were played with and
without it (`server/tools/ab_tactics.py`).

| Idea | Result | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| Declarer answers the four-kings signal with double game | +0.08 points per hand, clearly real | yes |
| Pagát ulti announcer draws tarokks with small ones | +0.08, likely real | yes |
| Take a trick a known opponent is winning | +0.05, uncertain | yes |
| Lead a suit a teammate is void in, avoid one an opponent is void in | +0.03, uncertain | yes |
| Feed points only into a certain trick | no measurable change | yes (safer) |
| Ulti announcer's teammate leads tarokks | no measurable change | yes (pagat advice) |
| Table treats the four-kings announcer as the partner | no measurable change | yes (it is the point of the signal) |
| Holder of the XX discards cheap cards | no measurable change | yes (pagat advice) |
| Play tarokk back to a partner in general | no change | no |
| Win every late trick | no change | no |
| Lead small tarokks from any long hand | −0.04 | no |
| Draw tarokks after the four-kings signal | −0.07 to −0.09 | no |
| Skíz held back to catch the XXI | no change | no |

The five from the first batch together gained about +0.11 points per hand
for the two seats using them. Not taken from the pagat page: invit bids,
yielded game, the 8/9-tarokk declaration and kontra tactics. The game has
no invit or tarokk declaration, and bots never kontra.

One observation for you: the four-kings signal itself costs points when the
kings are not all captured, which is most of the time (about 160 of 170
signals in the simulation). The declarer's double-game answer wins some of
that back. If you would rather signal for free, the engine would need a
separate "I have many tarokks" declaration, as the 8/9-tarokk announcement
does in the pagat rules.

## Deviations from the plan

- Seats on screen: I sit at the bottom, the next player is on my right, then
  top, then left (play runs counter-clockwise). The old client drew the next
  player on the left.
- Server work for seats, next hand and the shared action layer went in as
  one change instead of three.
- Hungarian was written together with English from the start; the Hungarian
  task became a review.
- "Play one hand in four browser tabs" was done as one person against three
  bots.
- Cards picked for the skart stay in place in the hand (lifted) and are
  mirrored in the slots on top; in the mockup they left the hand.
- Card pictures are WebP with transparency, not PNG: 2.4 MB for the deck
  instead of roughly 15 MB.
